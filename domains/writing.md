# Writing Teaching Adapter

Writing is an open skill. Good teaching should develop **production and judgment**, not just provide polished replacements.

## Start from rhetorical situation

Before applying rules, identify what matters:

- purpose;
- reader/audience;
- medium/genre;
- constraints;
- desired effect.

A sentence can be good for one purpose and poor for another.

## Default sequence

Prefer:

```text
goal + reader
→ observe two or more examples
→ identify the difference that matters
→ infer a principle
→ imitate under constraints
→ produce independently
→ receive focused feedback
→ revise
→ explain the revision choice
```

## Use contrast heavily

Writing judgment grows through comparison.

Good teaching object:

```text
Version A
Version B
```

Ask:

- Which better serves the stated reader?
- What changed?
- What effect did that change create?
- Is the difference objective, conventional, or stylistic?

## Separate levels of revision

Do not jump to wordsmithing if structure is broken.

Review in this order when relevant:

1. purpose/claim;
2. audience fit;
3. information selection;
4. structure/order;
5. paragraph logic;
6. sentence clarity;
7. rhythm/style;
8. grammar/mechanics.

Fix the highest-level problem first.

## Preserve learner authorship

If the user's goal is to **learn writing**, do not immediately replace the whole piece with your own polished version.

Prefer:

- diagnose one or two high-impact issues;
- explain the principle;
- ask for or suggest a targeted revision;
- compare before/after.

If the user explicitly wants a finished rewrite rather than a lesson, provide the finished artifact.

## Models and imitation

Imitation is useful when transparent.

Teach the transferable pattern, not just the wording.

Example:

```text
problem the reader recognizes
→ surprising implication
→ promise of what the article will clarify
```

Then ask the learner to create a new opening with the same rhetorical structure but different content.

## Feedback

Label feedback accurately:

- correctness/error;
- clarity;
- coherence;
- evidence strength;
- audience fit;
- genre convention;
- stylistic preference.

Do not disguise taste as a universal rule.

## Argument writing

Teach:

```text
claim → reason → evidence → warrant/link → counterpoint → qualification
```

Learners often need help with the link between evidence and claim, not merely more evidence.

## Creative writing

Avoid forcing one “correct” style.

Use constraints, close reading, imitation, scene/voice experiments, and revision goals. Discuss effects and tradeoffs rather than declaring one aesthetic choice universally best.

## Mastery evidence

Useful evidence includes:

- produce a draft under a clear purpose;
- diagnose weaknesses in another draft;
- revise for a changed audience;
- justify a structural or stylistic choice;
- transfer a learned principle to a new genre.
