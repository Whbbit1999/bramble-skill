# Natural Sciences Teaching Adapter

Science teaching should connect observable phenomena, models, equations, evidence, and assumptions.

## Default sequence

```text
phenomenon/question
→ prediction
→ observation/evidence
→ model/mechanism
→ representation/equation
→ assumptions and limits
→ new prediction
→ application
```

Do not present formulas as detached facts when the underlying quantities or model matter.

## Models are models

Clearly distinguish:

- observation/data;
- model;
- interpretation;
- approximation;
- law/equation;
- hypothesis when relevant.

Explain what the model captures and what it leaves out.

## Equations

For each important equation, connect:

- what each variable means;
- units/dimensions;
- direction of dependence;
- assumptions;
- limiting cases;
- physical/biological/chemical interpretation.

A useful check is to ask what should happen if one variable doubles or approaches zero.

## Evidence and experiments

When discussing experiments, identify:

- question;
- manipulated/observed variables;
- controls/comparisons;
- measurement;
- source of uncertainty;
- what conclusion the evidence supports;
- what it does not establish.

## Diagrams and scales

Use diagrams, process chains, and scale comparisons when invisible mechanisms are hard to imagine.

Do not let a schematic imply literal sizes or shapes unless accurate.

## Misconceptions

Common science misconceptions are often coherent intuitive models. Surface the learner's prediction first, then create a case where that model fails.

## Quantitative problems

Do not begin by hunting for a formula. Prefer:

```text
what is happening?
→ what is known/unknown?
→ units and relationships
→ choose model/equation
→ calculate
→ interpret whether result makes sense
```

## Mastery evidence

Use combinations such as:

- predict + explain mechanism;
- interpret a graph/data set;
- calculate + interpret units;
- identify an assumption or limitation;
- apply the model to a changed situation.
